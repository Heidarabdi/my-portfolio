Hardware fails, software crashes, and networks slow down. Being an IT Support Specialist at a major institution like **Jamhuriya University of Science & Technology (JUST)** is about more than just fixing broken printers—it's about ensuring the continuity of education.

## Proactive vs. Reactive Maintenance

The biggest shift I implemented was moving from a "break-fix" model to a proactive maintenance schedule. Instead of waiting for a computer to fail during a crucial exam, we established a weekly audit routine.

- **Weekly:** Software updates and antivirus scans.
- **Monthly:** Disk cleanup and hardware dust removal.
- **Semester:** Full system reimaging to clear temporary student data.

## Standardization is Key

Managing over 200 workstations individually is impossible. We utilized disk cloning tools to create a "Golden Image"—a master configuration with all necessary drivers and software installed.

## Troubleshooting under Pressure

When a projector fails five minutes before a lecture, panic is the enemy. I developed a quick triage protocol:

1. **Verify physical connections:** Is it plugged in? Are cables secure?
2. **Isolate variables:** Swap the cable, then the laptop, then the port.
3. **Software check:** Are drivers updated? Is the output source correct?

These experiences taught me that technical skills are only half the job; communication and calmness under pressure are what truly define a successful IT professional.
